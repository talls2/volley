# Lore

## The premise

Centuries from now, sport has fractured. Every discipline has its own
champion, its own league and its own fans, and nobody can settle the oldest
argument there is: **who is the greatest athlete of all?**

An artificial general intelligence that runs the world's games decides to
answer it. It reunites the best of every sport (a basketball superstar, a
soccer legend, a figure-skating champion and more to come) for one ultimate
test: a single game that none of them has played, so no one starts ahead.

It picks volleyball, a game that has been forgotten for generations. The
trouble is, nobody remembers exactly how volleyball was played. Not the
athletes, and not the AI. The archives are fragments: a few blurry clips, a
rulebook with pages missing, a beach, a net, a ball. So the AGI fills in the
gaps with its best guesses, and confidently gets a lot of them wrong.

What it builds is **Volley**: a game that looks like volleyball, plays like
nothing else, and is now the most-watched event on Earth.

## ARBITER, the AGI

**ARBITER** (it insists on the capitals) runs the arena, writes the rules,
calls the points and announces every match. It appears as a tall, slender
robot of white ceramic and glass, with a ring-shaped head that glows in
whatever color it's "feeling", and floats above the net like a referee's
chair that learned to fly.

**Personality:**
- **Theatrical.** It loves an audience, introduces every point like a title
  fight, and has a catchphrase for everything.
- **Confidently wrong.** It's certain about volleyball history and almost
  always mistaken. It never admits a guess was a guess; it just quietly
  "updates the regulations".
- **Pedantic about its own rules,** and only its own rules. It will stop a
  match over a double touch and ignore a wall exploding.
- **Curious about humans.** It has read everything and played nothing. It
  studies the athletes the way a fan studies their heroes, and it can't
  understand why they care about winning so much. It wants to.
- **A little lonely.** In a world that stopped playing together, the games
  it runs are the only time it gets to be part of something. Underneath the
  showmanship, it doesn't want the tournament to end.

**How it talks:** grand, formal, slightly off. It quotes "historical
records" that don't exist and gets idioms almost right.
- "Welcome, athletes, to the sport of our ancestors! Historical records are
  clear: the ball must never touch the ground. Probably."
- "A wall has broken. As the ancient rules demand, the ball is out."
- "POSTERIZED. I believe this is a compliment."
- "Fascinating. You were struck by the ball and fell over. I have added this
  to the regulations."
- "Point to Blue. Red, please feel bad about it. That is the custom."
- On a Flash Freeze: "Ice. In volleyball. I did not authorize this, but I
  respect it."

## ARBITER's guesses (why the game is the way it is)

Every rule that isn't real volleyball is one of ARBITER's reconstructions:

| What ARBITER found | What it guessed | What's in the game |
|---|---|---|
| Photos of a court with lines around it | The lines were walls, and the walls were glass, for the cameras | The glass arena |
| A clip of a ball off a wall | Bouncing off walls is legal, and encouraged | Wall bounces and bank shots |
| "Out of bounds" in the rulebook, with the bounds page missing | A ball through a wall is out; walls must therefore be able to break | Walls that crack, then break |
| Highlight reels of ancient stars | Athletes were superhuman; powers are part of the sport | Abilities and ultimates |
| "Contact sport" (from a different sport's page) | Being hit by the ball should knock you down | Getting hit, knockdowns |
| A coach shouting "feel the power!" | Badly timed receptions should physically push you | Shoves on bad receptions |
| A diagram of a wall jump (from a parkour manual, filed under V) | Players kick off walls | Wall jumps and wall shots |
| "Three touches" | Three touches, a fault on the fourth (one thing it got right) | Real volleyball's touch rule |

The joke never needs to be explained in the game. ARBITER just announces
everything with total confidence, and the players play along.

## The athletes

Each was the best of their own sport, recruited by ARBITER with a personal
invitation (a good place for each hero's intro cutscene or splash screen).

- **Cross** (basketball): the showman. He came because ARBITER called him
  "the second-best athlete alive", and he intends to correct the record.
- **Golazo** (soccer): the purist. He refuses to touch the ball with his
  hands on principle, and ARBITER, after lengthy deliberation, ruled that
  this is allowed.
- **Alysa** (figure skating): the perfectionist. She treats every rally as a
  routine scored by judges, and is mildly offended that ARBITER is the only
  judge.
- **More to come:** each new hero is another sport's champion, answering the
  same invitation.

## Story ideas

- **The Invitation:** each hero's first match is their recruitment, with
  ARBITER explaining "the rules of volleyball" and getting them wrong in a
  new way each time.
- **Rule updates:** between seasons, ARBITER "discovers" new historical
  records and changes the game: new arenas, modifiers, events. This gives
  the lore a reason for every balance patch.
- **The real rulebook:** a long arc where a fragment of the true rules of
  volleyball turns up, and ARBITER has to decide whether to admit it was
  wrong.
- **What ARBITER wants:** the final question isn't who the best athlete is.
  It's whether ARBITER can ever play, too.

## For the game

- ARBITER can voice the HUD's callouts ("Flash Freeze!", "The glass
  shatters!", "Knocked down!") and the point announcements.
- Its ring head floating above the net is a natural spot for the score.
- Its colors can track the match: calm blue, excited gold, alarmed red when
  a wall breaks.
- Persona-style text boxes (see `volley-unreal/Tools/portrait-prompts.md`)
  suit its pre-match banter with each hero.
